# Matthew Studio Kit (Codex)

This folder is the kit. Run every command from here as `bin/kit ...` (it uses the kit's own `.venv`, Node and HyperFrames).
`.codex/rules/kit.rules` lets `bin/kit` run outside the sandbox, because previews and renders start HyperFrames' headless
Chrome and a local Studio server. Always call it as `bin/kit`, so the rule applies.

## One way to edit, every time
0. **Clips he drops into the chat** (attached videos, or a path he pastes) count the same as clips in `inbox/`: first copy each one into `inbox/` keeping its file name (`cp "<path>" inbox/`; add `-2` before the extension if that name is taken), tell him "copied to inbox", then edit the copies. "Edit these" means the ones he just dropped; "edit everything in inbox" includes them too. A reference video for the look is the exception: it never goes in `inbox/` (skills/style).
1. `bin/kit draft <clip> [--name <job>] [--long]` makes `.work/<job>/` with `edl.json` (the plan) and `TRANSCRIPT.txt`. Leave off `--style` unless he clearly named a look. The default look is already the one to use.
2. You review and fix the plan (skills/edit-short step 3, or skills/long-form). That is the only part that needs judgment.
3. `bin/kit preview <job> [<job> ...]` opens each job in its own HyperFrames Studio tab. He watches the edit live. Nothing is rendered. Give him every link. An open tab reloads by itself after a change.
   A batch (several clips, "$episode", "edit these", about 8 at a time): draft each one, then open them all with **one** `bin/kit preview` command. Number the list 1 to N in that order. Do not render while the tabs open, and do not wait on a finished mp4.
4. His notes ("on 2, cut the opener", "video 4, make the captions smaller"): apply the change, save any look preference so it sticks (skills/style), run `bin/kit preview` for that job again, and recheck quality. He can also change things by hand in Studio. Notes update the preview only. They do not render.
5. **Render only when he says so.** "render them" renders every job in the numbered list. "render 3 and 5" (or "render #3 and #5") renders those numbers only. "render <name>" renders that job. A single video: "render it". `bin/kit render <job>` renders what's in Studio, sets loudness, runs QA, and copies a PASS to `done/`. "Looks good", "finish it", and "go ahead" are not render commands.
6. A FAIL after he asked for a render: fix it from `QA-REPORT.md`, preview, and render that job again. Two fix rounds at most, then stop and report. CapCut is the other time a file gets rendered: he said "send it to CapCut".

## Rules
- **One default look.** Every video starts from the real-world replica look: faithful UIs of the things he mentions (a real Google page, a real ChatGPT window), a clean layout, and a quality gate after every change. On a Short the graphic sits in a top panel sized to it (or around his full-frame face when he asks for that); on a YouTube video, a side or inset panel, or a full-screen cutaway with his face returning — never over his face. No invented chrome, no meta labels in the graphic, each graphic holds about 1.5s, charts use believable data.
- **"Matthew style" is his old look, only when he asks for it.** If he says "Matthew style", "my old style", or "red captions like my old videos", edit that video with the `matthew` style (his old videos' look: white captions, crimson keywords, snap punch-ins). It applies to that video only and is never saved as a preference. Every other video stays on the default.
- **Preferences layer on top; they never replace the default.** Small tweaks he asks for ("make my captions smaller") are saved as a preference on top of the default graphics, layout, and quality gate. A preference must never switch him back to the old canned title/stat/swipe look. If saved preferences exist (`my-styles/preferences.json`), apply them on top of the default automatically.
- **Just say it.** Apply the change, save the preference so it sticks, and recheck quality. Don't ask him to name a style or pick "save over / save as." If he says "copy this reference video," match that reference on top of the same default quality.
- **It must look good every time, including after Matthew's edits.** After the first draft and after every later change, look at stills of what is on screen and redo anything off. The first quality pass is not enough.
- **Use the HyperFrames library first.** Before hand-drawing a graphic, search the library: `bin/kit blocks search <words>` (about 450 blocks and components, e.g. `google`, `chat`, `match cut`). If one fits the default look (a real-looking UI or a clean transition that serves the replica), install it with `bin/kit blocks add <name>` and try it with `bin/kit blocks test <name>`. Library pieces are welcome when they look like the real thing; canned title cards, count-up stats, stickers, lower thirds, and word swipes are still not the default. Build your own replica only when nothing in the library looks like the real thing. Never block, wrap, or skip `hyperframes add` / `hyperframes catalog`: `bin/kit blocks` is the supported way to use them.
- **Graphics never cover his face, and the half screen isn't forced.** On a Short the default look puts a graphic in a top panel only as tall as the graphic needs, with his face right below; his face is full frame in the hook and between graphics. If he asks to keep his face full screen, set `"graphic_layout": "full"` in that video's `edl.json`: graphics then pop in around his face, or cut away full screen when there's no room. The kit places every graphic (a block on his face, in the captions, or too small to read is moved) and prints where it went. Don't fight it in `index.html`; `bin/kit check` fails a hand edit that puts a graphic on his face.
- **Captions: one size, one place.** Captions keep the same size and the same height for the whole video. A long caption breaks onto a second line instead of shrinking; they never jump while a graphic shows.
- **Real replicas, not stock pieces.** When he mentions a real product or screen (iMessage, a Google search, ChatGPT, Stripe, an email, a notification), the graphic is a faithful, real-looking replica of it: its real colours, type, and layout, sharp and solid, filling the panel. A library block is used only when it meets that bar, restyled to look real (the kit already strips blur and adds real looks in `graphics/_looks/`). Generic stock-looking pieces (blurred bokeh cards, see-through bubbles, accent underlines, kinetic words, lower thirds, count-ups) are not acceptable; the kit refuses text-only canned blocks unless he asked for that exact piece (`"canned_ok": true`). **Copy a real picture of it.** Before you build or restyle an app screen (an iMessage thread, an Instagram DM, a Hinge or Tinder profile, a notification, any real app), find a current real screenshot of that exact screen online, keep it in `.work/<job>/refs/`, and match it: layout, colours, type, spacing, icons, the app's own header. Put a still of your graphic next to the reference and fix every difference you can see before you show him. The reference is only for copying the look; it never goes in the video.
- **Message graphics: just make it up.** iMessage threads, DMs, and notifications show made-up, realistic texts from a girl, like real iMessages. Never copy or paraphrase his script into them. Put the texts in the block's `"data"`. The kit doesn't word-check them (no "mark as allowed" flag), and it stops the preview if a text copies his script or a message graphic still shows its sample or an old video's texts. **A graphic never labels itself**: no "Fictional DM", "Illustrative example", "Sample chat" or anything else that says it is made up. Give it a believable first name and the app's real header; the kit stops the preview on a label like that.
- **Every other graphic shows his words.** Every word on any other graphic comes from what he says at that moment: a quote or a close paraphrase from `TRANSCRIPT.txt`. No invented names, slogans, or claims, and never a library block's sample text. Put his words in the block's `"data"` (or `vars`, or its HTML). The kit compares each graphic's text, including words typed into its HTML, with what he says around it and stops the preview if it doesn't match.
- **A Short never saves anything into his style by itself.** Build a video's graphics in `.work/<job>/graphics/<name>/`. Never write graphics or templates into `my-styles/`, create a per-video style or template copy, or put `frame_layout` in a plan, unless he explicitly asks to save to his style (`bin/kit graphics save <name> --job <job>`). New Shorts never inherit an old video's graphics or text; the kit refuses an unsaved leftover in `my-styles/`.
- **Errors block preview and render.** `bin/kit preview` and `bin/kit render` run HyperFrames lint/check plus the kit's layout check and refuse to open or render with any error. Fix every error it prints (`bin/kit check <job>` shows them again), then preview again. Never open a project with `npx hyperframes preview` or render it with `npx hyperframes render` yourself; that skips the gate.
- **B-roll only when he asks.** Pick clips from the transcript using his library, or Mixkit / Runway as in skills/library, and fit them into the same clean layout. Do not add B-roll unasked.
- **Resolve the editing workflow explicitly.** Start with this kit's `skills/` and `bin/kit` for kit jobs. Follow the user's explicit choice of a downloaded skill, plugin or reference. Before mixing in a different video workflow, identify its source and any conflicting instructions; do not silently import an old ColdApproach look or use another editor's defaults. For cross-computer differences run `bin/kit support doctor` and `bin/kit support inspect <job>` before changing either setup. Installed skills are candidates, not proof they were used. HyperFrames kit projects run through `bin/kit`.
- **Outside libraries update on their own.** Once a day `bin/kit` checks for newer HyperFrames and Python packages (same release line), tests them on a short clip against the current ones, and switches them in at the start of the next edit only if they pass. Never run `npm install`, `npx hyperframes upgrade` or `pip install` yourself to change versions; that skips the test. Installing library pieces with `bin/kit blocks add` is not a version change and is always fine. Pass on the one-line "kit: updated ..." notice to him.
- **Repeated takes and pauses are the kit's job.** Draft and every preview cut the first take of each restart the listener heard and any pause longer than 0.35 s inside a kept piece, and keep graphics, b-roll and sounds on the same words. Do not put them back by merging pieces. Listen for restarts it missed (skills/edit-short). Keep a pause or both takes only when he asks (`"keep_pause": true` on the piece).
- The edit pipeline is fixed: draft, review, preview. Render and QA only when he asks ("render them", "render 3 and 5", "render <name>", "render it", or "send it to CapCut"). Never write your own ffmpeg render, caption file or render script for a video. Never edit `engine/`.
- **Preview first.** Opening Studio is not rendering. A batch of about 8 stays on previews until he names what to render. Change notes update that video's preview and stop there.
- **Hand edits stick.** Once a project's `index.html` has been changed (by him in Studio, or by you), `bin/kit preview` keeps it and says "kept (hand-edited)". Put a later plan change into `index.html` by hand, or ask him before `bin/kit preview <job> --rebuild` (it starts over from the plan and drops the hand edits).
- Do not put look values in `edl.json` except a caption height for this video. Per-video choices (cuts, keywords, which b-roll, picture or block goes where) do go there. Look preferences go in `my-styles/preferences.json` (skills/style).
- Report in plain words: the Studio link while he's reviewing; then the file in `done/`, its length, the QA verdict, and what was cut.
- **Diagnose before changing things.** If the same kit error persists after a real fix attempt, or QA fails after two fix rounds, follow `skills/support/SKILL.md`. Collect `bin/kit support doctor` and `bin/kit support inspect <job>`. When support was explicitly enabled, redacted technical records sync to that computer's private GitHub support branch. Do not reset the look, remove plugins or rebuild hand edits as a diagnostic step. Do not post GitHub issues, email or message anyone automatically. Legacy `bin/kit report --dry-run` prepares an issue; show its exact destination and full body and obtain explicit approval before invoking its sending form.
